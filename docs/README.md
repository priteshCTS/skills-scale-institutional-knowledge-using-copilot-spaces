# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project-management model that starts with initiation and continues through planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement. The process is designed to align stakeholders, clarify ownership, and measure progress against concrete outcomes. It emphasizes clear roles, visible communication, and incremental delivery so the team can deliver value without losing alignment on scope, quality, or risk.

Project work begins with a lightweight initiation phase to define the business need, stakeholders, success metrics, and high-level milestones. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning, where the backlog is prioritized, estimates are created, acceptance criteria are defined, dependencies are identified, and timelines are mapped. This ensures the work is actionable and the team has a shared understanding of what success looks like before execution begins.

During execution, OctoAcme relies on regular project rhythms such as standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and PR-based review workflows. The operating model reinforces small, reviewable changes, CI validation, and quality gates before merge. The team also follows clear risk and communication practices to surface blockers early, escalate issues appropriately, and keep stakeholders informed with a single source of truth. Quality is maintained through unit, integration, and smoke testing, plus security scans and manual QA where needed.

The lifecycle culminates in release preparation, deployment verification, and retrospectives that capture lessons learned and convert them into action items. Roles are clear and intentional: Project Managers coordinate delivery and communication, Product Managers define outcome and priority, developers build and validate the solution, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Together, these practices create a repeatable project-management approach for delivering value with clarity, accountability, and continuous improvement.
